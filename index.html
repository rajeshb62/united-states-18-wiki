A narrative-research knowledge base on American history from the aftermath of the French and Indian War through the early national period, covering the American Revolution, the founding of the federal government, the development of the early republic, and the lives of the principal figures who shaped the new nation.

  • [source] Alexander Hamilton (Chernow, 2004) — Ron Chernow’s comprehensive biography of Alexander Hamilton, covering his origins in the West Indies, role in the American Revolution, founding of the American financial system, and his fatal duel with Aaron Burr.   • [source] Benjamin Franklin: Une vie américaine (Isaacson, 2003, French ed. 2017) — Walter Isaacson’s comprehensive biography of Benjamin Franklin, translated into French.   • [source] Life and Times of Benjamin Franklin (Parton, 1864; Houghton Mifflin 1897) — James Parton’s two-volume mid-Victorian biography of Benjamin Franklin, from Northamptonshire ancestry and Boston apprenticeship through the London agency, Congress, the French mission, peace, the Pennsylvania presidency, and the Convention of 1787, with documentary appendices.   • [source] Report on Public Credit (Hamilton, 1790) — Alexander Hamilton’s Report on Public Credit, submitted to the House of Representatives on January 9, 1790 — his foundational plan for funding the national debt, assuming state debts, and establishing public credit. Read source page
  • [source] Report on a National Bank (Hamilton, 1790) — Alexander Hamilton’s Report on a National Bank, submitted to the House of Representatives on December 13, 1790 — his plan for the Bank of the United States, including the theoretical justification of banking, the specific $10 million capital plan, and the response to constitutional objections. Read source page
  • [source] Report on the Establishment of a Mint (Hamilton, 1791) — Alexander Hamilton’s Report on the Establishment of a Mint, submitted to the House of Representatives on January 28, 1791 — his plan for a national coinage system answering six specific questions: the money unit, the gold-silver ratio, alloy composition, coinage costs, denominations and devices, and the phase-out of foreign coin. Read source page
  • [source] On the Constitutionality of a National Bank (Hamilton, 1791) — Alexander Hamilton’s opinion on the constitutionality of the bill for establishing a Bank of the United States, submitted to President Washington on February 23, 1791 — the definitive argument for broad construction of the Constitution.
